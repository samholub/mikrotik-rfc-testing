# Buy used NIDs first, bench smart SFPs later

A smart SFP cannot yet give this ISP a more accurate acceptance test than `rfc-test.rsc`. A pair of used carrier NIDs probably can, for under $200, and a DIY TRex box is the cheapest way to prove it on the bench. Most smart SFPs only reflect traffic. Of the SFP-form products that do generate, the one with the best documentation (Cisco's Assurance SFP, formerly Accedian's) runs Y.1564 **two-way only** and needs Cisco's Sensor Control, an orchestrator and a licence for every configured test. The only smart SFP that claims **one-way (per-direction) Y.1564** is PlumSpace's. That claim rests on a marketing page, and the company's country of origin is unclear. No source found shows anyone running any smart SFP in a MikroTik cage, and the RB4011 is documented as rejecting 1G copper SFPs, the class a smart SFP with an SGMII host side falls into. Used Adva FSP 150-GE114Pro NIDs, by contrast, have eBay asking prices of about **$30-75 each**. Their datasheet lists an embedded RFC 2544 generator and analyzer, Y.1564, and **"ITU-T Y.1731 single- and dual-ended frame loss"**, with no licence wording. They sit inline as boxes, so the SFP-cage question does not arise. Carrier NIDs are the right first buy, as the hardware-counted control the repo's conventions already require. The bench must first show that their dual-ended loss counts match injected drops in each direction, and that they reach 64 B at line rate. A smart SFP earns bench time only if its vendor answers basic questions in writing. A caution runs through everything below: every price is an eBay asking price seen through a search index on 2026-09-30, not a verified sale, and no product here has a published MikroTik interoperability record.

## Most smart SFPs reflect traffic, and only three families claim to generate it

The distinction that matters is whether the module can originate a test stream and count what comes back, or can only turn around someone else's stream. A reflector-only pair cannot run an acceptance test by itself. Both ends would be responders, and RouterOS has no Y.1564 generator to drive them.

**Generators.** The **Cisco Provider Connectivity Assurance Sensor SFP** (1G SX/LH/copper and 10G SR/LR/ER/BiDi; formerly the Accedian Skylight "SFP compute" and "Nano") lists "RFC2544 generation and reflection" and "Y.1564 generation and reflection", runs up to four SAT tests, and claims "SAT testing at line rate for any packet size with 1-usec delay measurement accuracy" ([Cisco datasheet](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/products/collateral/cloud-systems-management/provider-connectivity-assurance-sensors/provider-connect-assurance-sensor-sfp-ds.html)). Its documented frame range is **64 to 10,240 B** ([Crosswork Assurance docs](https://docs.crossworkassurance.cisco.com/skylight/docs/using-rfc-2544-for-traffic-generation-and-analysis.md)), which makes it the only smart SFP with a documented 64 B floor. The **PlumSpace Smart SFP Ethernet Tester** advertises "BER", "RFC 2544" and "Y.1564, 4 streams, 1-way, 2-way", with generation "up to 10 Gbps" ([PlumSpace](https://plumspace.com/products/smart-sfp-ethernet-tester/)). Its IP SLA Probe claims a "Line rate packet generator and analyzer" ([PlumSpace](https://plumspace.com/products/smart-sfp-ip-sla-probe/)). **RAD's MiNID** was a responder in its 2013-2016 datasheets. The later 2.6 datasheet adds that it "can also initiate Layer-3 SAT with its own generator" and lists a "TWAMP Light controller and responder" ([MiNID 2.6 datasheet](https://www.pulsesupply.com/images/pdf/rad/minid_2_6_ds_ga.pdf)).

**Reflectors, or unclear.** **VIAVI's Fusion JMEP** (1G and 10G) is positioned everywhere as a loopback for VIAVI test sets ("Activates test loopbacks (L2/L3)"), although one brochure sentence mentions "Y.1564 traffic generation" ([VIAVI](https://www.viavisolutions.com/en-us/products/fusion-jmep); [brochure](https://www.viavisolutions.com/en-us/literature/fusion-jmep-smart-sfp-brochures-en.pdf)). Treat it as a reflector until a manual says otherwise. The **OE Solutions/AimValley** smart SFP's own brief calls its test role "Service Activation Test Loopback for Y.1564 and RFC2544" ([product brief](https://www.smartsfp.com/documents/Ethernet_OAM_productbrief.pdf)), and the current product overview no longer lists the Ethernet OAM part ([smartsfp.com](https://www.smartsfp.com/products-overview/)). PlumSpace's NID and Loopback models are reflectors ([PlumSpace NID](https://plumspace.com/products/smart-sfp-nid/)). The **Cisco Smart SFP NID** (ME 1200 family) is retired: its datasheet URL now lands on Cisco's retired-products page ([Cisco](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/products/collateral/switches/me-1200-series-carrier-ethernet-access-devices/datasheet-c78-734249.html)). Ciena, Adtran/ADVA, Canoga Perkins, Albis, EXFO and Juniper Paragon all do Y.1564 in boxes or software agents, but none of them sells an SFP-form tester. Juniper's hardware agents are deprecated in favour of NFX150 and ACX ([Paragon docs](https://app.netrounds.com/static/2.24/support/testagents/tech-info/hwspecs.html)).

### Four earlier claims that do not survive the notes

**"Accedian Antentry" does not exist.** An exact-phrase search turned up only Accedian's "ant" module ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accedian); [Fastech](https://www.fastech-india.com/nanonid-ant/)), which Light Reading describes as "an iPhone-sized device that sits behind the cable modem" ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/cable-technology/accedian-steps-up-cable-business-drive)). That is a box, not an SFP. Accedian's real SFP line is the Nano/SFP compute, now sold as the Cisco Assurance SFP after Cisco bought Accedian in 2023.

**"$100-200 smart reflector SFPs"** rests on a single listing: a used RAD MiNID (part 6790020000) asking **$175.49**, reduced from $194.99, with "9 sold" in the snippet ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/114401834096)). That part number may be the sleeve or standalone variant rather than the SFP, and MiNID also needs a software option ordered alongside the hardware. An Accedian Nano lists at $22.99 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/325193256622)), but it is only useful with Cisco's controller.

**"$800-2,500 new" has no source.** No new or distributor price was found for any smart SFP. They are sold on request-for-quote terms. The only PlumSpace price found in the press is £150 for a 2022 TAP-style module, not the Ethernet Tester ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2022/01/18/plumspace_smart_sfp_tap/)).

**Loopback plugs are not test instruments.** Passive "loopback connectors" exist ([Precision OT](https://www.precisionot.com/product/loopback-connectors/)), but a physical loop swaps no MAC or IP addresses and counts nothing. Operators use them as the far end for a hardware tester on a VLAN dedicated to testing ([reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/networking/comments/qwu5wv/)), and in the one MikroTik thread where a plug appears, the plug served a Digital Lightwave tester and the MikroTik was the device under test ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/143125)). A plug gives round-trip loss only, and only to a tester that the ISP would still have to own.

## Only two mechanisms can name the failing direction

A loopback or reflector test measures the round trip. Every frame crosses the circuit twice, and loss in either direction lands in one number, so the result **cannot say which direction failed**. EXFO and VIAVI say this about their own loopback modes, and it is why they sell dual test sets ([EXFO EtherSAM](https://www.exfo.com/contentassets/2f87303d99c244ab8d1928eefe9837af/exfo_anote230_ethersam-ethernet-service-testing_en.pdf); [VIAVI](https://www.lasercomponents.com/fileadmin/user_upload/home/Datasheets/viavi/nitro/2-enhanced-rfc-2544.pdf)). Running a loopback test from each end does not help, because each run is still a round trip. Per-direction loss needs one of two things. The first is a **one-way generator/analyzer pair**: A sends a known stream, and B counts it in hardware. The second is **dual-ended Y.1731 loss measurement** between two MEPs, where each end counts the service frames it receives, and the two ends exchange counters, so near-end and far-end loss come out separately.

The documented products sort cleanly by this test. **Cisco's Assurance SFP is round-trip for SAT.** Its RFC 2544 generator "must pair ... with another device that loops the traffic back", and its Y.1564 supports "Layer-2 two-way testing" and "Layer-3 two-way testing" only ([RFC 2544 docs](https://docs.crossworkassurance.cisco.com/skylight/docs/using-rfc-2544-for-traffic-generation-and-analysis.md); [Y.1564 docs](https://docs.crossworkassurance.cisco.com/skylight/docs/using-y1564-for-service-activation-testing-1.md)). A pair of them gives hardware-counted round-trip loss, nothing more. **Adva's older ECPA workflow** also needs "a remote loopback at the far end" ([FSP 150CCf-825 manual](https://fccid.io/ANATEL/00374-11-01746/Manual/94D53F27-C39A-42E9-9058-067C4323D029/PDF)). No source fetched documents a one-way generator-at-A, analyzer-at-B mode for Adva ECPA, RAD ETX or Accedian SAT. An earlier assumption that a used NID pair runs "dual test set" mode out of the box is therefore **unverified**, and the bench has to settle it.

The dual-ended Y.1731 route is better documented. The Adva GE100Pro series datasheet (GE112Pro/GE114Pro) lists **"ITU-T Y.1731 single- and dual-ended frame loss"** and synthetic loss measurement alongside its generator ([Adtran datasheet](https://www.adtran-networks.com/-/media/adva-main-site/resources/data-sheets/pdfs/fsp-150-ge-100-pro-series.pdf)). RAD MiNID lists Y.1731 loss on "synthetic and real traffic" ([MiNID DS v2.3](https://www.rlcomunicaciones.com/docs/rad/fo/MiNID.pdf)), and OE/AimValley lists Y.1731 frame loss ([OE Solutions](https://oesolutions.com/blog/oe-solutions-and-aimvalley-add-y-1731-to-smart-sfp-portfolio/)). The distinction between the two Y.1731 flavours matters. **Synthetic loss (SLM) samples a low-rate probe stream**, so it cannot see 0.10% loss in a line-rate load. **Service-frame LM counts the real frames passing through the MEPs**, so if a test load (a NID generator, or btest itself) runs through a pair of NIDs, dual-ended LM should report that load's loss per direction from hardware counters. That is an inference from datasheet wording, and it is the single most valuable thing the bench can check.

**PlumSpace is the only smart SFP that claims one-way results**: "Y.1564, 4 streams, 1-way, 2-way", with jitter "Symmetrical and asymmetrical" ([PlumSpace](https://plumspace.com/products/smart-sfp-ethernet-tester/)). Treat it as unverified. No datasheet or manual was retrieved. Nothing explains how two units pair, how the far end's counts get back to the operator, or whether loss is counted in the FPGA or the CPU. The CPU matters because independent teardowns found PlumSpace's Linux path far below line rate: about 125 Mbps on an older unit ([benjojo](https://blog.benjojo.co.uk/post/smart-sfp-linux-inside)), about 190 Mbps in 2020 ([NetBeez](https://netbeez.net/blog/smart-sfp/)), and about 900 Mbps on a 2025 dual-core A53 unit ([apalrd](https://www.apalrd.net/posts/2025/network_smartsfp/)). Line-rate testing therefore depends entirely on an undocumented hardware engine. Provenance is also unsettled. LinkedIn places the company in Ajman, UAE ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/plumspace)), while an independent blogger calls it a "Russian company" ([benjojo](https://blog.benjojo.co.uk/post/smart-sfp-linux-inside)). Clear that before any purchase.

A **DIY TRex box** is the one option with unambiguous one-way counting, but only on the bench. With both of its ports wired around the device under test, it counts at its own receive port, so it measures loss per direction with NIC counters ([TRex manual](https://trex-tgn.cisco.com/trex/doc/trex_manual.html)). It cannot do that across a real circuit without a second box at the far end, and TRex has no two-box coordination.

## The RB4011 cage is the first gate, and nobody has tried one

Power is the smallest worry. MikroTik publishes no per-cage budget, but it states that "all the current MikroTik devices with an SFP+ cage support the S+RJ10", which averages **2.7 W** ([MikroTik](https://help.mikrotik.com/docs/spaces/ROS/pages/240156916/S+RJ10+general+guidance)). 1G smart SFPs draw well under that: Cisco's 1G optical draws 1.75 W and the MiNID sleeve 1.2-1.68 W. The Cisco 10G parts, at 2.6-3.0 W, and its 1G copper part, at 3.0 W, sit at or above the S+RJ10 envelope ([Cisco datasheet](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/products/collateral/cloud-systems-management/provider-connectivity-assurance-sensors/provider-connect-assurance-sensor-sfp-ds.html)). PlumSpace publishes no power figure at all. The hEX S family is the riskiest host. It is not listed for the S+RJ10, and a 2026 report describes an ONU-type SFP boot-looping a hEX S (2025) on cold power-up ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/271297)).

The host interface is the real gate. The RB4011 manual says it "does not support passive DAC modules, 1GB copper SFP modules and SFP GPON modules" ([RB4011 manual](https://help.mikrotik.com/docs/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=33849352)). Its SFP+ port connects straight to the Annapurna SoC ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/131675)), and a GPON stick that works in an RB2011 fails in it. MikroTik cages accept a module that presents 1000BASE-X, SGMII or 10G PCS, but the RB4011 is "picky ... Especially for 'exotic' ones" ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/155705)). **No vendor fetched states whether its smart SFP presents 1000BASE-X or SGMII to the host.** A copper-line variant almost certainly presents SGMII, so only 1G *optical* smart SFPs belong on the RB4011 bench. MikroTik's guidance for 1G optics in SFP+ cages is auto-negotiation off and speed forced to 1G full duplex ([MikroTik compatibility](https://help.mikrotik.com/docs/spaces/ROS/pages/220233794/MikroTik+wired+interface+compatibility)).

Management works in-band, the same way as the GPON sticks MikroTik users already run. That means a VLAN interface on the SFP port with an address in the module's subnet ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/178110)). RAD adds "Loaned IP" management that borrows the host's address ([MiNID DS](https://www.rlcomunicaciones.com/docs/rad/fo/MiNID.pdf)). Cisco's module expects Accedian discovery on VLAN 4001 with Ethertype 0x88fc and a Cisco controller ([Plug and Go note](https://docs.crossworkassurance.cisco.com/docs/plug-and-go-application-note-140)). One trap is documented: with no light on the line side, RouterOS marks the port no-link, and the module becomes unreachable unless `sfp-ignore-rx-los=yes` is set, a setting that was broken on the hEX S until a 2026 fix ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/175620)). The VLAN interface, the address, the forced speed and the RX-LOS setting are all router configuration. The script must never change configuration, so they belong in a documented one-time install step in the runbook, not in `rfc-test.rsc`. RouterOS should also read a smart SFP's DDM like any module ([MikroTik Ethernet](https://help.mikrotik.com/docs/spaces/ROS/pages/8323191/Ethernet)), but on an RB4011 a clean DDM readout has coexisted with a dead data path ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/139158)).

The evidence stops there. **No report was found, from any source, of a RAD, Accedian/Cisco, VIAVI, OE/AimValley or PlumSpace smart SFP running in a MikroTik, Ubiquiti or white-box host.** The only MikroTik forum mention is a 2017 suggestion to try them ([forum](https://forum.mikrotik.com/t/6185)). Anyone who buys one is the first to document it.

Two things follow from this. First, the smart-SFP market has **almost no used supply and no public new prices**, and the best-documented generator (Cisco) is effectively unusable without an enterprise assurance stack. ## Buy two used GE114Pros first, and make the bench prove them

**Step 1, about $100-250: a pair of used Adva FSP 150-GE114Pro** plus a console cable. Estimated from the asking prices: about $80-200 for the pair with shipping, plus the cable. They are the best-documented cheap route to hardware counting at both ends. The generator, Y.1564 and dual-ended Y.1731 loss are all on one datasheet with no licence caveat, they have local CLI and web management, and as inline boxes they avoid the RB4011 cage entirely. A used RAD ETX-203AX pair is the fallback, *after* the seller confirms the unit is licensed for 1000 Mbps. Before either result appears in an acceptance record, the bench must prove five things:

1. After a factory reset, and with no vendor contract, ECPA and Y.1564 start and report from the local CLI or web UI.
2. Dual-ended Y.1731 LM reports per-direction loss that matches `nth`-injected drops in each direction at test rates. Inject at 10% and at about 0.1%, with raw per-flow counters as ground truth, the way the 2026-09-25 btest records were made. The injection point must sit between the two NIDs, so one RB4011 has to act as the "carrier" across two of its ports.
3. Whether an analyzer at B accepts and counts a stream generated at A. That is the undocumented one-way mode.
4. Whether the NIDs forward and count 64 B frames at 1G line rate (1.488 Mpps) with zero loss on a clean path. The datasheets say only "wire speed".
5. That the round-trip hardware loss on the same path equals the sum of the two inbound `lost-packets` totals from running `rfc-test.rsc` once from each end. This calibrates the script's ~93% capture against hardware for the first time.

**Step 2, optional, about $15-70 plus a spare PC: a TRex box** with an X520-DA2 or ConnectX-4 Lx. Its job is not field testing. It is an independent 64 B line-rate source with true one-way counting, so the NIDs, and later any smart SFP, can be checked against something other than themselves. TRex's reference platform is a dual-socket server, and "Fewer [DRAM] channels will impose a performance issue" ([TRex book](https://github.com/cisco-system-traffic-generator/trex-core/blob/master/doc/trex_book.asciidoc)). An SFF desktop is plausible at 1G but undocumented at 10G/64 B, so the box's own capacity is also a bench question.

**Step 3, bench-first and conditional: PlumSpace, and only PlumSpace.** It is the one SFP-form product that could deliver per-direction loss without a controller. Contact the vendor only after the NIDs have shown what a trustworthy result looks like, and buy a single pair only if PlumSpace answers in writing: the host-side mode (1000BASE-X or SGMII), power draw, minimum frame size, whether loss is counted in hardware, how one-way results reach the operator, and the country of incorporation. Specify 1G optical parts, never copper, for the RB4011. On the bench the pair must pass everything in step 1, plus link-up with auto-negotiation off at a forced 1G, cold-boot survival, management reachability with the far end dark, and temperature under sustained load. **Skip the Cisco Assurance SFP**, because it is two-way-only for SAT and bound to a controller. Skip JMEP (it needs a VIAVI test set), OE/AimValley (a loopback, apparently de-emphasised), and MiNID except as a far-end Y.1731 MEP or responder for another generator.

None of this changes `rfc-test.rsc`. Under the repo's own rule, no script change or new verdict source is justified until a bench record exists. Any management setup a device needs (VLAN, address, forced speed) is a one-time router step documented in the runbook.

## Conclusion

The question assumes the script's main weakness is its one-end blindness to outbound loss. Running it from each end already closes that. Each run counts its own inbound loss to about 0.10%, so two runs cover both directions. What the script lacks is **provenance**: software counting that catches about 93% of drops, a CPU that can make a result inconclusive, and no 64 B frame. That changes what is worth buying. A **round-trip hardware test is useful even though it cannot name a direction**. If its hardware count matches the sum of the two btest runs' inbound loss, the software per-direction split is corroborated by hardware. That meets "escalate only what both ends agree on, after a known-good control" without buying anything that claims one-way magic.

The smart-SFP idea fails for this owner less on physics than on ecosystem. The products that generate traffic are either tied to a controller (Cisco, VIAVI) or unverified (PlumSpace), and the one attractive feature, per-direction loss, is better documented in a $40 used NID's datasheet than in any SFP's. If the Adva pair proves dual-ended LM on the bench, the practical form of "hardware per-direction loss" at this ISP is two inline boxes plugged in for turn-up and taken away afterwards, not a module left in a MikroTik cage.
